# ADR-010 — Hiring invitations and consent-based messaging

Status: accepted (2026-10-27). Builds on ADR-009 (discovery) and ADR-003 (isolation).

## Decisions

**What an invitation is.** An employer's expression of hiring interest in a person who chose to be found, for one open, published job of that employer. It is not an application, an employment offer, a shift hold or a booking, and it never reserves availability. It carries the business name, role, location, disclosed pay and super wording, hours and dates when known (permanent/temporary kind and duration are kept separate from the legal engagement basis), a note of at most 500 characters and an expiry (default 7 days, 1–14). The terms are snapshotted with a hash.

**Sending (`POST /v1/employers/{id}/invitations`).** Recruiter or owner of a verified employer. The person is named by the opaque per-employer reference from search; the server resolves it only against `talent_exposures` (people this employer was shown in the last 7 days), so another employer's reference, a guess, a raw id or an old result resolves to nothing. At send time everything is re-checked: master and per-kind discovery consent, enabled kind, invitations not paused, active account, no `hide_from`/`block`, the job still open. Every candidate-side refusal returns one generic `candidate_unavailable` so an employer cannot tell consent withdrawal from a block or a quota. Quotas (employer/day, job/day, recipient/day) and the employer-wide 30-day cooldown after a decline are counted in Postgres under advisory locks (employer then recipient), so teammates and several API instances share them. A partial unique index forbids two live (pending/accepted) invitations for the same employer, candidate and job; `Idempotency-Key` is unique per employer and altered payloads under a key conflict.

**Lifecycle.** pending → accepted / declined / withdrawn / expired. Every transition locks the row (`FOR UPDATE`), re-reads the status and writes one terminal state, audited. Expiry is enforced on read (effective status), on every action (committed before the refusal is returned) and by a worker sweep. Accepting requires the `terms_hash` the person reviewed to equal the job's current terms; if the job changed they are shown what changed and must accept the latest terms (the original snapshot is kept; the accepted terms are recorded separately). A closed job cannot be accepted. A repeated accept returns the same conversation.

**Conversations.** Exactly one per accepted invitation (unique on `invitation_id`). Nothing is possible while an invitation is pending, declined, withdrawn or expired. Plain text (1–2000 chars), server timestamps, newest-first cursor pages, `client_key` idempotency per sender, per-user rate limit, a single "new message" nudge per conversation until read. Acceptance shares no contact details, resume or calendar. No read receipts or presence are exposed (unread counters are per side and never shown to the other).

**Ending versus pausing versus blocking.** *Pause invitations* (`invitations_enabled`) stops new invitations only. *Withdraw discoverability* (ADR-009) also stops new invitations. *End conversation* closes one thread for both sides. *Block* closes every thread with that employer, removes the employer's read access to them (`employer_access=false`, history kept for the candidate and support), declines pending invitations, silences nudges, removes the person from that employer's search and refuses new invitations. Removing a team member takes effect on the next request because membership is checked per request and RLS uses it.

**Notifications.** The notification row written in the same transaction is the durable outbox; the existing worker pushes it after commit and marks it `pushed_at` (at most once; a provider outage never undoes the invitation or double-sends). Text is generic ("You have a new invitation / message"); details need an authenticated fetch. Push respects the candidate's push setting and removes dead device tokens. Deep links open `/invitation/[id]` or `/conversation/[id]`, which fetch with the current account's token; another account's id is a 404 and caches are cleared on every sign-out.

**Discovery budget.** An employer can be shown at most 200 distinct people per rolling 24 h (atomic, shared). Residual limit: a budgeted employer can still probe up to that many people a day; this narrows, it does not remove, the free/not-free side channel, and no anonymity is claimed.

**Data.** Forced RLS on invitations, conversations, messages, reports and exposures. Export includes invitations, the person's threads (both sides' text) and their reports. Deleting a candidate account erases invitations, conversations and messages (employers lose those threads; whether employers must retain hiring records is an open legal question). Only a minimal report record survives, detached from the reporter, with the reported excerpt, for abuse handling; that retention needs counsel sign-off and is separate from marketing consent. Logs carry ids and statuses, never message text, notes, pay preferences or tokens.

## Known limits
Employer-side push (no native employer app) and email/SMS are not built; attachments are deferred until scanning exists; support/moderation tooling has no UI (reports are rows); `employer_access` hides a blocked thread from the employer but does not remove it from backups; no device or real-push verification has been done.
