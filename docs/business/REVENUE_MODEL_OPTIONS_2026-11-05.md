# NUA CREW — Employer Revenue Model Options
**Date:** 5 November 2026 (Melbourne)
**Status:** Pricing foundations are owner-directed; all new products below are proposals, **not authorised for activation**.
**Market:** Australian hospitality first. NZ remains preview-only with no NZD transactions.
**Principle:** Job seekers stay free. Employer charges pay for transparent workflow/visibility value, not private worker data, compliance status, or preferred access to protected candidates.

Persist server-generated integer-cent quotes and use a versioned catalogue. Historical 1900/4900-cent orders retain their original amount and settlement/refund rights; do not reprice them.

## 3. How to optimise revenue ethically
1. **Establish marketplace liquidity before increasing monetisation pressure.** Test meaningful applicant supply per hospitality category, response time, hires per ad, and employer repeat-use. A$49 must buy an honest opportunity to reach suitable workers, not a promise of applications.
2. **Keep pay-as-you-go prominent.** Employers should not need a subscription for one role; the 14-day, one-time A$49 offer is the conversion anchor.
3. **Sell workflow improvement, not applicant access.** Paid reporting, seat collaboration and scheduling are defensible; private CVs, availability, contacts or personal data must never be sold or unlocked merely by payment.
4. **Use quantity packs before adding complex recurring subscriptions.** Limited packs can validate employer willingness to pay with less billing complexity.
5. **Promotions remain capacity-limited and clearly labelled.** Do not promise top-five placement where slots or relevant audience are unavailable; no pay-to-be-verified.
6. **Offer a practical value guarantee only after pilot data.** If a paid ad cannot be delivered or fails moderation, provide statutory refunds/credits as appropriate; do not invent guaranteed hiring outcomes.
7. **Measure revenue *net* of fees and trust costs.** Track paying employers, conversion from free preview, ad repurchase, promotion attachment, subscription retention, support load, CAC and median days to first suitable applicant.

## 4. Pilot experiments (not launch requirements)
- A/B employer pricing explanation: simple A$49 one-time vs A$49 with explicit A$30 boost, **same price to all within an experiment** and clear checkout.
- Survey pilot venue owners on whether 5-ad packs or a recurring hiring desk makes sense after using the product.
- Measure time-to-first-application and employer follow-up before running paid acquisition campaigns.
- Revisit feature packaging only after 20+ interviews and enough cohort data to avoid false confidence.

## 5. External positioning reference
- SEEK Australia uses variable ad pricing by role, location and candidate demand; published product terms and ad-price guidance apply.
- Indeed Australia offers some free posts and results-based sponsored billing; NUA CREW should compete on transparent one-time pricing and hospitality-specific workflow rather than claim universally cheaper pricing.
